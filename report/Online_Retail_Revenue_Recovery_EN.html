Online Retail: Revenue Reconciliation & Customer Value

Analysis of 541k real online-retail transactions: quantify the return/cancellation leak from "recognized revenue vs net revenue" (-$0.92M), and use customer repurchase/CLV models to put operations effort on the most valuable customers.

Part 1 · Business Objective

1. Why this analysis

A UK online retailer (real transaction data) faces 541,909 lines / 25,900 invoices / 4,372 customers. Two core business questions:

  • Revenue reconciliation: on-book "recognized revenue" is $10.67M, but returns/cancellations/refunds cause -$0.92M leakage (8.6%) — where does it leak, and which SKU/country/month is worst.
  • Customer value & churn: top 10% of customers drive 60% of revenue; losing a high-value customer hurts most — use models to identify who will churn vs who will buy again, and spend operations effort on the most valuable customers.

One-line goal: plug the return/cancellation leak via revenue reconciliation, and retain high-value customers / warn of churn via customer-value models.

Reconciliation boundary. This project completes transaction-layer revenue reconciliation: quantifies the return/cancellation gap between recognized and net revenue ($0.92M). Line-level payment/refund matching needs bank or platform statements, not in this dataset — listed as next step, not claimed done.

Part 4 · Modeling

4. Business-driven customer modeling (principle · why · how)

4.1 Goal: predict "will the customer buy again" + "how much"

Why (business-driven). A rule only splits customers into high/low value by historical spend; it cannot judge who will churn next year. Using 2010 RFM features, models predict 2011 repurchase (classification) and spend (CLV regression) — so operations puts "churn warning + outreach" on likely-to-churn, meaningful-amount customers.

Principle. Learn customer-history(RFM) → future-behavior mapping: baseline logistic/linear regression (interpretable coefficients), then random forest / gradient boosting (interactions, e.g. "high frequency × high returns × low value").

How & leakage control. All features come from 2010 (spend, frequency, days-since-last, return amount); all labels from 2011 (repurchase, future spend) — temporal split, labels never leak into features. 948 feature-period customers, stratified 75/25 by repurchase status.

Repurchase PR-AUC is high (0.92+) because positives dominate (86.5% repurchase); AUC ~0.63 is medium. For CLV, linear wins (R² 0.32); trees overfit (small sample). Model choice: on repurchase GBM (AUC 0.627) and logistic (0.628) differ little — GBM is chosen on a slightly higher PR-AUC (0.925) and tree extensibility; for CLV, linear regression is used because it wins (R² 0.32) and trees overfit.

Honest read. Repurchase AUC ~0.63 is a medium signal: repurchase is 86.5% (imbalanced, too many positives) and only 948 feature-period customers. The model works for ranking churn risk (put the most-likely-to-churn first) but is not a precise amount forecast — CLV R² 0.32 explains only part of the variance. The report does not overclaim "accurate prediction"; it is an auxiliary signal for operations tiering. No deep learning: 948 samples, no sequence; trees/linear already fit and stay explainable; DL adds little at this scale and hurts the "customer ops must be explainable" narrative.
